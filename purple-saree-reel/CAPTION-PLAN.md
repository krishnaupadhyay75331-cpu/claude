## 2. Niche and message

- **Niche:** devotional and lifestyle commentary for Hindi-speaking married women. It's a festival opinion piece, not the relationship-consultant niche the brief assumed.
- **Problem she names:** Karwa Chauth is turning from a vrat into a trend: designer lehenge, reels, photoshoots, a "mehendi ki race", fasting *dikhane ke liye* (for show).
- **Her position / offer:** shringaar is fine and part of parampara, but the real strength of the vrat is **shraddha**. Couples who give each other samman, samay, izzat and pyaar make the most beautiful Karwa Chauth, and couples now fasting together set an example of pyaar and **barabari**.
- **Experience, numbers, offers, personal name:** none said. See `PLACEHOLDERS.md`.
- **Call to action:** "Aapki nazar mein Karwa Chauth vrat hai ya trend? Comment mein zaroor likhein, follow karein [Maa Chamunda Jyotish]."
- **Red emphasis (2 allowed):** **Dikhane** (the pain word) and **Shraddha** (the promise word).

## 3. Beat map vs. the brief

The brief's beat order holds: hook with B-roll → greeting → problem montage → "why it happens" → method/values → authority → happy-ending B-roll → CTA. Where her content differs, the treatment moves with its beat:

- **Greeting:** "Jai Maa Chamunda" in Great Vibes script, in place of "Namaste" and the name pill (no name is spoken).
- **Side cut-outs:** B&W phone/social-post cut-outs (her problem is social media), in place of an arguing couple.
- **Callout tags:** the four values she names, in place of services.
- **Stat title:** **BARABARI**, the fallback claim word, because no number is spoken.
- **B-roll #7 (happy ending):** the brief says "no captions", but her key question "vrat hai ya trend??" plays over it. It's the engagement hook, so it gets two stacks.
- **CTA:** her own "Comment mein zaroor likhein" on the end freeze, in place of "Click the link Below".
